01 / START WITH THE BASICS

Good records.
Good habits.

You don't need the most expensive setup to enjoy vinyl. Start with equipment that treats your records well, learn a few care habits, and collect at your own pace.

Collecting should be fun, not a race. Set a budget, make a want list, and leave room for the records you didn't know you were looking for.

02 / YOUR FIRST SETUP

The starter kit.

Three essentials to help you get started without overcomplicating the process.

01

A reliable turntable

Look for a stable, adjustable setup with a replaceable stylus. Avoid treating built-in novelty players as a long-term setup.

02

Basic cleaning tools

A carbon-fiber brush for everyday dust and a record-safe cleaning method for deeper cleaning will go a long way.

03

Smart storage

Store records upright, away from heat and direct sunlight. Use clean inner sleeves and don't pack shelves too tightly.

03 / HANDLE WITH CARE

Make every
spin count.

  • Hold records by the edges and label area.
  • Brush away surface dust before playing.
  • Keep records in their inner sleeves when not in use.
  • Store them vertically in a cool, dry place.
  • Keep your stylus clean and replace it when worn.

04 / NEED-TO-KNOW

Frequently asked questions.

A few quick answers for the beginning of your collecting journey.

Do I need an expensive turntable to start?

No. Start with a dependable turntable that has a replaceable stylus and a properly supported tonearm. Prioritize a solid setup over extra features you may not use.

How should I clean my records?

For light dust, use a carbon-fiber record brush before playing. For deeper cleaning, use a product specifically made for vinyl and follow its instructions. Avoid household cleaners.

What's the best way to store vinyl?

Store records vertically, not in leaning piles. Keep them away from direct sunlight, heat, moisture, and crowded shelves that can warp sleeves or records.

How can I tell an original pressing from a reissue?

Compare the catalog number, label details, country, release date, and runout or dead-wax etchings. Discogs release pages can help, but verify details against the copy in your hands.

How do I keep collecting on a budget?

Make a want list, set a price ceiling, check condition carefully, and explore used bins. Reissues can be a great way to enjoy an album without paying original-pressing prices.
